# v1.1 Wide-angle lens in the camera view

## Goal

The camera view opens the phone's default rear camera (1x). Most phones also carry an
ultra-wide lens (0.5x or 0.6x in the native camera app), and for a large sheet, a poster, a
whiteboard or a page on a table the user cannot step back from, 1x does not get the whole
document into the frame. v1.1 lets the user switch to the wide lens while aiming and back,
where the browser offers one. Where it does not, the camera view looks exactly as in v1.0.1.

The web does not reach the lens the way the native camera does. Chrome on Android exposes the
wide lens as a zoom range below 1 on the one logical rear camera; Safari on iOS exposes it as
a second video input device; some Android phones expose neither. The feature therefore probes
both and hides itself when nothing is found (decision 2026-09-23, see below).

## Scope

In:
- Probe: after the default stream has started, `src/camera.ts` determines the available lens
  options (`lensOptions`). Two mechanisms, tried in this order:
  1. Zoom range (Chrome on Android): `track.getCapabilities().zoom` with `min < 1`. The wide
     lens is `applyConstraints({ advanced: [{ zoom: min }] })` on the running track, the
     default is `zoom: 1`. No restart, the stream keeps its size.
  2. Second device (Safari on iOS, some Android): `enumerateDevices()` after the permission
     grant lists the rear video inputs. The device whose stream is *not* the current one and
     that reports the widest view is the wide lens. Labels are localised ("Rückkamera
     (Ultraweitwinkel)" / "Back Ultra Wide Camera"), so the choice is not by string: iOS
     lists the physical rear cameras in the order ultra wide, wide, telephoto, followed by the
     virtual dual/triple cameras, and the current track's `deviceId` marks the wide (1x) one;
     the entry right before it is the ultra wide when there are two or more physical rear
     cameras. On phones whose order is unknown the probe gives up rather than guessing.
     Switching stops the current stream and starts a new one with
     `deviceId: { exact }` (no `facingMode`), keeping the ideal size.
  Neither found: no button.
- Button: a switch (`switchButton`, `role="switch"`, `aria-checked`) with the wide-angle icon
  left of the shutter, mirroring the detect switch on the right; `aria-label` "Weitwinkel".
  On = wide lens. It is hidden (not disabled) while the probe has found nothing, while the
  camera is starting and during a switch.
- Behaviour on switch: the frozen still is released (v0.12), the live detector is stopped and
  restarted on the new stream, the static frame is laid out anew from the new stream size
  (`layout`), and the outline returns to grey until the tracker finds the document again.
  The capture pipeline (`captureStill`, strict detection on the still, bake) does not change;
  it reads the session's size, whatever the lens.
- Session state: the lens choice lives in the app state like the detect toggle and is kept
  across camera opens until the app is closed, so a user scanning several large pages does
  not switch on every [+]. Each camera open starts the default stream and then applies the
  remembered choice; if applying fails (device gone, constraint rejected) the choice resets to
  the default, the switch shows off, and no notice is shown: the picture is still there.
- Errors during a device switch are handled like a camera start error: the error screen with
  its cause label, retry reopens the default lens.
- Backgrounding (the visibility handler) restarts the camera as before, which reapplies the
  remembered lens.

Out:
- Telephoto or any third lens: two states only, default and wide. A lens picker would be a
  list, and text.
- Continuous zoom (a slider): the frame concept relies on the user framing the page; a zoom
  slider is a second way to do the same and competes with the pinch zoom of the edit views.
- Lens choice in the still: the page has no notion of which lens took it. The wide lens has
  more distortion in the corners and, on iPhones, no autofocus; the perspective correction
  handles the geometry and the text sharpness is what it is. The user sees it in the captured
  view and can retake.
- Persistence. Nothing is written; the switch resets when the app is closed.
- A hint that the wide lens exists. The switch appears where it works; that is the hint.

## Behaviour

1. `openCamera` starts the default stream as today. Once it is attached the view calls
   `probeLenses(session)`, which returns `null` (no button) or a `LensControl` with
   `select(lens: 'default' | 'wide'): Promise<CameraSession>`. The zoom variant resolves
   with the same session; the device variant stops the old stream, starts the new one on the
   same video element and resolves with a new session.
2. The camera view renders the switch when a `LensControl` exists and reflects the app
   state's `lens`. Tapping it: hide the switch, release the frozen still, stop the live loop,
   `await select(...)`, replace the session, `layout()`, restart the live loop if the detect
   toggle is on, show the switch in its new state.
3. Taps during a switch are ignored (the switch is hidden). A shutter press during a device
   switch finds no session and does nothing; during a zoom switch the session is live and the
   press captures at whatever zoom the track is at, which is acceptable: the still is what the
   user saw.
4. Camera open with `lens === 'wide'` remembered: start default, probe, `select('wide')`,
   then show the view as ready. The switch shows on. Failure of the select: log, set `lens` to
   `'default'`, continue with the default stream.
5. Camera close stops whatever stream is current; nothing about the lens survives except the
   app state's `lens`.
6. The static frame keeps its rule: DIN portrait rectangle fitted into the visible part of the
   cover-cropped video. A wide stream with a different aspect ratio (iOS ultra wide streams
   are 4:3 like the main camera; Android zoom keeps the size) changes the visible part and
   `layout` recomputes the frame.

## Data model changes

`src/model.ts`: none. App state (`src/app.ts`) gains `lens: 'default' | 'wide'`, session
state, default `'default'`. `src/camera.ts`: `probeLenses`, `LensControl`, the two
implementations (`ZoomLensControl`, `DeviceLensControl`), and `startCamera` takes an optional
`deviceId`. `src/camera-view.ts`: the lens switch, `setLensControl`, `onLens` callback. No
stored data.

## Icons used

New: `wide-angle` (a wide-angle lens glyph: a lens circle with two short diverging rays, or a
landscape frame with outward corner marks), in `intent/icons/wide-angle.svg` and
`src/icons.ts`. Existing: `shutter`, `magic-wand`, `arrow-left`.

## Acceptance criteria

- Unit test (jsdom, stubbed `mediaDevices`): a track whose capabilities report `zoom.min 0.5`
  yields a `LensControl`; selecting wide calls `applyConstraints` with `zoom: 0.5` and keeps
  the session; selecting default applies `zoom: 1`.
- Unit test: a track without a zoom capability and `enumerateDevices` listing two rear inputs
  in iOS order yields a device control; selecting wide stops the first stream and calls
  `getUserMedia` with the other device's `deviceId` exact and no `facingMode`.
- Unit test: no zoom range and a single rear device yields `null` and the camera view renders
  no lens switch.
- Unit test (app shell): switching the lens releases the frozen still, restarts the live
  detector and relays out the frame; the switch is absent from the DOM while the select is
  pending and shows `aria-checked="true"` afterwards.
- Unit test: the lens choice survives closing and reopening the camera within a session; a
  rejected select on reopen falls back to default with the switch off.
- Unit test: a failing device switch shows the error screen with the unavailable cause, and
  retry opens the default lens.
- On the phone: iPhone with three cameras shows the switch and the wide view roughly doubles
  the field of view; a Pixel or Samsung with a zoom range below 1 does the same without a
  visible restart; a phone without either shows no switch and the camera view is unchanged.
  An A3 sheet at arm's length fits the frame with the wide lens and is captured, detected and
  rectified; the A4 flow at 1x is unchanged.
- Switching lenses twenty times in a row on iOS does not exhaust canvas memory (nothing is
  grabbed on a switch; the frozen still is released).
