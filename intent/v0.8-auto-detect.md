# v0.8 Auto-detect

## Goal

Both edit views get an [auto] button with a magic-wand icon. In the crop/rotate view it looks
for the paper edges close to the frame, from the frame inward, and presets the frame
(rectangle, rotation and, if needed, corner offsets) to what it found. In the brightness/
contrast view it switches to black-and-white, measures the paper background and the printed
text, and sets brightness and contrast so that the page becomes full black on white. In both
cases the result is a pending edit: the user accepts, corrects with the existing controls, or
backs out.

## Scope

In:
- Crop/rotate view: bar becomes [back] [crop|rotate|shear] [rotate 90] [auto] [confirm]. The
  auto result replaces the pending frame; the mode stays as it was (the frame is drawn as the
  detected quadrilateral in every mode since v0.7).
- Brightness/contrast view: bar becomes [back] [brightness|contrast|temperature] [grayscale]
  [auto] [confirm]. Auto sets grayscale on, temperature neutral, brightness and contrast from
  the histogram; the slider shows the value of the selected segment.
- Contrast range widens to 0.5 .. 4.0 for everyone (decision 2026-09-22): typical paper
  photos need a contrast of 2 to 3 to reach full black and white. The slider becomes
  piecewise-linear with the neutral value at the centre tick for all three values (this also
  centres the contrast tick, which sat at a third before).
- Detection restricted to the frame's neighbourhood: bands from 5 % outside to 20 % inside
  each frame edge (decision 2026-09-22: mostly inward, with a small outward band because the
  capture frame is 90 % of the visible image and the paper often extends a little past it).
- Feedback: when nothing usable is found the brief warning icon of v0.4 is shown and the
  frame or tone stays unchanged. Partial results (some edges found) are applied.

Out: full-image document detection, live detection on the camera view, OCR, deskew by text
lines, adaptive (local) thresholding, colour-preserving auto levels.

## Behaviour

Frame detection (`src/detect.ts`, pure maths on `ImageData`, unit tested)
1. Working copy: the frame region plus the outward band, rendered in frame-local
   orientation (drawn with the inverse of the frame rotation, so the frame is an axis-aligned
   rectangle in the working image), downscaled so the frame's longer side is about 800 px,
   converted to luminance. The 90° base angle is irrelevant here; everything happens in
   frame-local coordinates and is mapped back at the end.
2. Bands: for each edge, the strip from 5 % outside to 20 % inside the frame, measured along
   that edge's normal in fractions of the frame's size along the same axis.
3. Edge profile: within a band, a 3-pixel box blur along the edge direction followed by the
   derivative along the normal. Sample lines every 4 px along the edge; on each sample line take
   the position of the largest absolute gradient. Absolute, because the paper is normally
   brighter than the table but a white desk turns that around.
4. Line fit: repeated-median regression over the samples (robust to the spurious maxima where
   text or a shadow crosses the band). Accept the line when at least 60 % of the samples lie
   within 2 px of it and the mean gradient at the inliers exceeds a threshold of 12 / 255 per
   pixel. Otherwise the edge is "not found" and the current frame edge is kept as its line.
5. Corners: intersect the four lines (in working coordinates), map back to image coordinates.
6. Frame from corners: rotation is the mean of the two long edges' angles; if it is within the
   ±15° skew range around the current base it becomes the frame angle, otherwise it is clamped.
   Width and height are the means of opposite sides, the centre is the quadrilateral's centroid.
   The residual of each corner against that rectangle becomes the corner offset (v0.7). Offsets
   under 1.5 image pixels are dropped, so a straight-on scan yields a plain rectangle.
7. Not a single edge found: warning notice, frame unchanged.

Frame detection is synchronous, under 200 ms on a mid-range Android phone at 800 px, so it
runs without the busy overlay.

Tone detection (`src/detect.ts`)
1. Working copy: the inner 90 % of the frame region, downscaled to about 500 px on the long
   side, luminance histogram with 256 bins. The outer 10 % is skipped because it often holds
   table or shadow.
2. Background `w`: the histogram mode among the bins above 0.4, refined as the mean of the
   bins within ±8 of the mode.
3. Text `k`: the luminance at the 1st percentile of the cumulative histogram. If `w - k < 0.2`
   the page has no dark content (blank page, or a faint form) and `k` is assumed as
   `max(0, w - 0.5)` so that a later pen mark still maps to black.
4. Values: with the v0.3 formula `out = c * b * in + 0.5 (1 - c)` mapping `w -> 1` and
   `k -> 0` gives `b = 1 / (w + k)` and `c = (w + k) / (w - k)`. Both are clamped to their
   ranges; with contrast up to 4.0 the clamp only bites on very dim photos.
5. Result: `{ brightness: b, contrast: c, temperature: 0, grayscale: true }` as pending tone;
   preview updates through the CSS filter as with a slider move. Confirm and back are the
   v0.3 paths. Tapping auto twice gives the same result (it measures the source image, not
   the preview).

Slider mapping (`toneFraction` and its inverse in `src/tone.ts`)
- fraction `= 0.5 * (v - min) / (neutral - min)` below neutral,
  `0.5 + 0.5 * (v - neutral) / (max - neutral)` above. Brightness and temperature are
  symmetric already, so only the contrast slider changes visibly. The native range input keeps
  a linear 0..1000 scale and the mapping happens in the view.

Layout
- The crop/rotate bar now holds seven 44 px targets (five buttons and a three-segment
  control). On a 360 px viewport this needs the compact bar with 4 px gaps; if device testing
  shows it does not fit, the auto button moves to the top-right corner of the stage (like the
  camera back button sits top-left) in both edit views.

## Data model changes

None in the model; `TONE_RANGES.contrast.max` becomes 4.0. New module `src/detect.ts`.

## Icons used

New: `magic-wand`. Existing: all of v0.7 and v0.3.

## Acceptance criteria

- Unit test: a synthetic image with a bright quadrilateral on a dark background and a frame
  that overlaps each paper edge within the band yields corners within 2 px of the truth; with
  a 5° rotated rectangle the detected angle is within 0.5°.
- Unit test: a paper edge 30 % inside the frame (outside the band) is ignored; the frame is
  unchanged and the notice is shown.
- Unit test: a synthetic two-level image (background 0.7, text 0.25) yields tone values under
  which the lookup table maps the background to 255 and the text to 0.
- On device: a page photographed on a dark desk with the frame roughly around it snaps to
  the paper edges after one tap; a page photographed at an angle yields displaced corners
  that confirm warps to a rectangle.
- Contrast slider: the neutral tick is at the centre; dragging to the right end gives 4.0 and
  the baked image matches the preview (regression of the v0.3 tests with the new range).
- Auto in the tone view followed by back leaves the image unchanged; followed by confirm bakes
  exactly the previewed values.
- All bars fit on a 360 px wide viewport with 44 px targets and no horizontal overflow.
