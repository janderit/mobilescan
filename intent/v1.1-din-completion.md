# v1.1 DIN completion for bound pages

## Goal

A page in a spiral block defeats the detection of v0.8 and v0.10 on one side: the bound edge
is a row of holes and wire, the sample lines along it scatter between hole rims and the paper
edge, and the line fit fails. The wand keeps the frame line there, and the live detection,
whose strict rule wants all four edges, never turns green. The other three edges are found
cleanly. v1.1 completes such a detection: when exactly three edges were found, the fourth is
inferred from the DIN A aspect ratio, parallel to its opposite edge, and accepted only if it
lands in the search band of that side, i.e. where the user aimed the frame anyway. This works
for a binding on the left, the right, the top or the bottom alike, because the missing side
is simply whichever edge did not fit.

The completion is behind a session flag (`settings.completeDinEdge`, on) so it can become a
user setting later; the maths never reads the flag itself (decision 2026-09-23, see below).

## Scope

In:
- `completeDinEdge` in `src/detect-edges.ts`: pure step on an `EdgeDetection` with exactly
  three edges found. The extent between the two adjacent found edges is measured at the
  opposite edge and at the frame line of the missing side and averaged; the two candidates
  (extent times sqrt 2, extent divided by sqrt 2) give two positions for the missing edge,
  parallel to the opposite one; the candidate nearer to the frame line wins. Accepted when it
  lies in the missing side's band (`BAND_INSIDE` to `BAND_OUTSIDE` of the frame line), otherwise the
  detection is returned unchanged. The result carries `completed` (the side) and its corners
  come from the four lines like any other detection.
- `DetectOptions` in `src/detect-frame.ts`: `detectFrameDetailed`, `detectFrame` and
  `detectFrameStrict` take the options last; with `completeDinEdge` the completion runs before
  the corners are formed. `FrameDetection.completed` reports the inferred side. The strict rule
  accepts four found edges or three plus a completion; the wand applies the completed edge
  instead of keeping the frame line (a partial result as before, only better placed).
- `src/settings.ts`: the session settings object with `completeDinEdge: true`. In memory,
  never persisted. `detectOptions()` in `src/detect.ts` turns it into `DetectOptions`, and
  `detectFrameIn` passes them to every rule, so the wand, the live loop and the still after
  the shutter all see the flag.

Out:
- Any user interface for the flag: it is on, and a switch or a settings page is a later
  version if the device tests ask for one.
- Other aspect ratios (letter, A5 in an A4 frame with a different ratio, receipts): the only
  ratio known without a user setting is the frame's own, DIN A.
- Two or fewer edges found: nothing to infer from; the rules behave as in v0.10.
- A fourth edge found in the wrong place (the hole rims fitting as a line): four edges found,
  no completion, no change against v0.10.

## Behaviour

Frame detection (`src/detect-edges.ts`, `src/detect-frame.ts`)
1. Edges as in v0.8: four bands, sample lines, repeated-median fit, `found` per side.
2. With the flag on and exactly three sides found: the missing side is completed. Let across
   be the axis the missing edge is searched along and along its own direction. The extent
   between the two adjacent found lines is read at the opposite line's position and at the
   frame line's position and averaged (the affine approximation of a perspective view; the
   error is within a few percent at ordinary tilt, and the PDF placement scales to A4 anyway).
   Candidates: extent times sqrt 2 and extent divided by sqrt 2 away from the opposite line,
   on the missing side, as a line with the opposite line's slope (a perpendicular distance
   `d` is `d * sqrt(1 + b^2)` across). The candidate nearer to the frame line is taken; it
   must lie in the band, else no completion.
3. Corners from the four lines, frame from corners, size and convexity checks as before.
4. The strict rule: `found === 4` or `completed !== null`, and not clamped. The wand: the
   frame with the completed edge.

The nearest-candidate rule resolves the orientation question (a page bound on top may be
portrait on its short edge or landscape on its long edge) without a user choice: the camera
frame is portrait A-format and the user fits the page into it, so the candidate that agrees
with the frame is the page the user framed. In the editor, a frame the user cropped to
landscape picks the landscape reading the same way.

## Acceptance criteria

- Unit test: a page bound on top (bright to the image border at the top, three clean edges)
  is completed on the north side; the strict rule accepts it and the inferred corners are
  within 3 px of the DIN position, the found corners within 2 px.
- Unit test: the same scene with the flag off is rejected by the strict rule, and the wand
  keeps the frame line.
- Unit test: a portrait page bound on the left is completed with the ratio divided by
  sqrt 2 (the short side missing).
- Unit test: a page 700 px wide bound on top, whose DIN edge would lie 22 % inside the frame
  line, is not completed; the strict rule rejects it and the wand keeps the frame line.
- Unit test: a page rotated by 4° and bound on the bottom is completed parallel to its top
  edge; all corners within 6 px of the truth.
- Unit test: the pure step returns null with four or with one edge found.
- Device test: a page in a spiral block bound on the left, then turned so the binding is on
  top, turns the outline green in the camera view and bakes to the page after the shutter;
  the wand in the crop/rotate view snaps the bound edge to the DIN position.
