# v0.6 Loupe previews while cropping and rotating

## Goal

While the user drags in the crop/rotate view, magnified previews of the affected frame corners
appear in the centre of the image, so it is possible to see whether the frame edge really sits
on the paper edge without lifting the finger or zooming.

## Scope

In:
- Loupes: round magnified views of the image around a frame corner, with the frame's edges
  drawn through them, shown only during an active drag in the crop/rotate view.
- Count follows what the drag affects: corner handle 1 loupe, edge handle 2 (the edge's two
  corners), frame body drag 4, fine rotation 4. In v0.7 a shear corner drag shows 1 and the
  body drag no longer exists (crop and shear were merged).
- Placement in the centre of the stage: one loupe centred; two side by side for a horizontal
  edge (n, s) and stacked for a vertical edge (e, w), so their arrangement mirrors the
  corners; four in a 2x2 grid in corner order (nw top-left, ne top-right, se, sw).
- The image inside a loupe stays still while the frame lines move, exactly like the main
  view; during rotation the lines turn inside all four loupes at once.

Out: pinch zoom, loupes in the brightness/contrast view, loupes on the camera view, loupes
that follow the finger (they would sit under it).

## Behaviour

Geometry
- Loupe diameter 96 CSS px, 12 px gap in a cluster, 3 px dark border with a 1 px white outer
  ring so it reads on both light paper and dark background.
- Magnification: 3x the view scale, capped so one image pixel is not stretched beyond 2 device
  pixels (on a downscaled 3 MP capture the loupe would otherwise show blur, not detail). At
  full capture resolution this shows about 100 x 100 image pixels per loupe.
- Each loupe is centred on its frame corner in image coordinates; the corner is therefore
  always at the loupe's centre and the two frame edges leave it in their true directions.
  The content is drawn with the same base rotation (multiples of 90°) as the main view.
- The frame lines in a loupe are the main view's frame lines mapped through the loupe's own
  affine transform (translate to corner, scale by magnification, rotate by base angle); an
  edge that passes through the loupe but does not end at its corner is drawn too.

Lifecycle
- Loupes appear on the first `pointermove` of a drag (not on `pointerdown`, so a tap shows
  nothing) and disappear on `pointerup`/`pointercancel`. No fade: they must not lag the
  finger, and under `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing would animate anyway.
- The cluster is centred on the stage. If the drag's start point lies inside the cluster's
  bounds inflated by 24 px, the cluster is shifted away from the finger for that drag:
  vertically first (above or below, whichever half of the stage the finger is not in), then
  horizontally; only when no shift keeps the cluster inside the stage is it not shown. A loupe
  under the finger helps nobody, but on a phone-sized stage the four-loupe grid plus margin
  covers most of the frame, so plain suppression left body drags and edge drags on a cropped-in
  frame without loupes most of the time (found in the first device test, 2026-09-22).
- Loupes never take pointer events (`pointer-events: none`).

Rendering
- Each loupe is a `<canvas>` sized to the diameter in device pixels (`MAX_DPR` cap as the
  stage canvas) inside a positioned `<div>` with `border-radius: 50%` and `overflow: hidden`;
  the frame lines are drawn on the same canvas after the image, so one element per loupe.
- Per `pointermove`: for each visible loupe, `drawImage` of the source region around the
  corner from `capture.image` (source rectangle of about 100 px, so the cost does not depend on
  the capture size), then the frame line strokes. Four loupes stay well below 4 ms on a
  mid-range Android phone; no work is done outside drags.
- The loupe canvases are created once with the view and reused; their pixel buffers are
  released together with the stage canvas when the view closes.

## Data model changes

None. The loupes read the pending frame and the display transform the view already keeps.

## Icons used

No new icons.

## Acceptance criteria

- Dragging a corner handle shows one loupe in which the frame corner sits exactly on the
  loupe centre; dragging an edge shows two; body drag and rotation show four in corner order.
- The frame line inside a loupe and the frame line on the main view meet the same image
  pixel (unit test: map a point on the frame edge through both transforms).
- With a loupe visible, dragging at the finger's speed shows no visible lag on a mid-range
  Android phone (per-move work under 4 ms measured with `performance.now()` in a dev build).
- A tap without movement shows no loupe. Loupes vanish on release, including `pointercancel`.
- A drag that starts within the centred cluster area shows the cluster shifted clear of the finger; on a stage too small for any shift it shows no loupes.
- Loupe contents follow a 90° tap: after the tap, a subsequent drag shows the corner content
  turned like the main view.
