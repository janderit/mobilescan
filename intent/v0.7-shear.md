# v0.7 Shear (perspective correction)

## Goal

A page photographed from an angle is a trapezoid, not a rectangle. A third mode in the
crop/rotate view lets the user pull each frame corner onto the corresponding paper corner
independently; confirm warps the image so that this quadrilateral becomes an upright
rectangle. As with crop and rotate, the image is not recomputed while dragging; the frame
shows the effect.

Terminology: the product calls it "shear". Geometrically, four independent corners define a
projective transform (homography), and that is what confirm applies. An affine shear could not
fix a trapezoid.

## Scope

In:
- Crop and shear are one mode (decision 2026-09-22, after the first device test: a separate
  crop-corner drag adds nothing that a shear corner cannot do). The segmented control stays
  [crop|rotate], the crop segment carries the `shear` icon and is the default. It shows all
  eight handles: the four corner handles move each corner on its own, the four edge handles
  crop the underlying rectangle as before. The frame body can no longer be dragged.
- The frame is drawn as the quadrilateral in both modes. Edges and rotation keep working on
  the underlying rectangle: an edge drag moves both corners of that edge along the rectangle's
  axis, rotation turns the whole quadrilateral, the 90° button turns it too.
- Confirm: if any corner is displaced, the whole captured image is warped by the homography
  that maps the quadrilateral to an upright rectangle, with white fill where no image lands
  (decision 2026-09-22: keep the margins, like rotation baking, rather than cropping to the
  quadrilateral). Rotation and shear are baked in one resample. Afterwards the frame is an
  upright rectangle with zero offsets.
- Warp engine: JavaScript pixel loop with bilinear sampling, in row strips, no WebGL
  (decision 2026-09-22: one code path, testable in Vitest, no GPU texture limits).
- The v0.6 loupe: a corner drag shows one loupe for that corner.

Out: automatic corner detection (v0.8), curved page flattening, aspect-ratio hints for the
result, a preview of the warped image before confirm.

## Behaviour

Model
- `Frame` gains optional `corners`: four offsets `[nw, ne, se, sw]` in frame-local
  coordinates (the rectangle's own axes, before rotation), each the displacement of that
  corner from the rectangle corner. Absent or all zero means rectangle. Only the pending
  frame inside the crop/rotate view ever carries non-zero offsets; `capture.frame` after
  confirm never does.
- Corner position in image coordinates: `fromFrameLocal(frame, rectCorner + offset)`. Because
  the offsets are frame-local, rotation, the 90° step (which swaps width/height and permutes
  the offsets `nw <- sw, ne <- nw, se <- ne, sw <- se`, turning each offset with the frame
  axes: `(x, y) -> (-y, x)`) and crop edge moves need no special treatment beyond the
  permutation.

Corner (shear) interaction
- Handles: four corners, 44 px targets, hit-tested against the displaced corner positions.
- A drag moves the offset of that corner by the pointer delta expressed in frame-local
  coordinates.
- Constraints, clamped not rejected: the quadrilateral stays convex; every edge stays at least
  10 % of the image width long; a corner may not be pushed outside the image beyond where it
  already is (same rule as crop: rotation may have put it outside, dragging may only bring it
  back in). Like a crop corner drag, the two frame-local axes are clamped one after the other,
  so a corner slides along an image edge instead of stopping where the drag path first hits it.
  The convexity and edge-length limits are not monotone along a drag path, so the clamp scans
  the path in 32 steps for the first invalid frame and bisects before it.
- The body cannot be dragged (the v0.2 pan was dropped with the merge; edges and corners
  cover every adjustment).

Edges and rotation with displaced corners
- Edge handles sit on the midpoints of the quadrilateral's edges and move the rectangle edge,
  keeping the offsets. The rectangle constraints of v0.2 apply to the rectangle; in addition
  the displaced corners obey the shear constraints above.
- The shaded area outside the frame follows the quadrilateral.

Confirm
- No offsets: the v0.2 path (`bakeRotation`, `drawImage`), unchanged.
- With offsets: the target rectangle has `width = mean(|top edge|, |bottom edge|)`,
  `height = mean(|left edge|, |right edge|)` of the quadrilateral in image pixels, so the
  pixel density of the result is close to the source's. The homography `H` maps the four
  quadrilateral corners to the target rectangle's corners (direct linear transform, 8x8 system
  solved by Gaussian elimination in `src/geometry.ts`, pure maths, unit tested).
- Output canvas: the bounding box of the warped image corners (`H` applied to the image's
  four corners), clipped to the target rectangle enlarged by 25 % of its width and height on
  each side and to the canvas pixel cap (`captureSize` logic). Strong perspective sends the
  far image corners towards infinity; the clip keeps the margin comparable to the capture
  margin instead. The canvas always contains the whole target rectangle, even where the
  quadrilateral stuck out of the image (white fill there, like a rotated frame in v0.2). White
  fill everywhere first.
- Resampling: `src/warp.ts` reads the source pixels once (`getImageData` of the whole image)
  and produces the output in strips of 256 rows: for every output pixel, `H^-1` gives the
  source position, bilinear interpolation of the four neighbours, outside the source leaves
  white. `putImageData` per strip. Target under 3 s for a 16 MP image on a mid-range Android
  phone behind the busy overlay (v0.4 deferral rules). Should a device fail to allocate the
  source `ImageData`, the bake retries at half resolution and the result is scaled to the
  target size; this is logged, not shown.
- After the bake, `capture.image` is replaced, the frame becomes the target rectangle
  (`angle` 0, no offsets) at its position in the output canvas, the old canvas is released.
- A frame with offsets whose quadrilateral happens to be a rectangle (all offsets zero after
  rounding) takes the v0.2 path.

## Data model changes

`Frame.corners?: [Point, Point, Point, Point]` (frame-local offsets). New module `src/warp.ts`
(homography resampling), homography maths in `src/geometry.ts`.

## Icons used

New: `shear` (quadrilateral with corner marks). Existing: `crop`, `rotate`, `rotate-90-right`,
`arrow-left`, `check`.

## Acceptance criteria

- Dragging one corner moves only that corner; the other three stay put.
- With displaced corners, dragging the right edge moves ne and se together along the
  rectangle's x axis; their offsets are unchanged.
- One 90° tap turns the quadrilateral with the image; four taps restore it exactly.
- Unit test: a synthetic image with a coloured trapezoid and a frame on its corners bakes to
  an image where the trapezoid is an axis-aligned rectangle of the target size (corner
  positions within 1 px, fill colour inside, white outside the source).
- Unit test: `H` maps each quadrilateral corner to its target corner within 1e-6; `H^-1 H` is
  the identity within 1e-9.
- Baking a 16 MP capture with all four corners displaced completes under 3 s on a mid-range
  Android phone and under 2 s on a current iPhone, with the spinner visible throughout.
- Confirm with zero offsets leaves the image bit-identical (regression of v0.2 behaviour).
- Back discards displaced corners together with the other pending changes.
